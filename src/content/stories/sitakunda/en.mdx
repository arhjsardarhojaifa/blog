Some places invite you to rest.

Some call you toward the unknown.

And then there are places that simply whisper—

**Come. Climb.**

Sitakunda is one of them.

Long before I ever set foot there, I had seen countless photographs of Chandranath Hill, hidden waterfalls, dense forests, winding trails, and clouds resting gently over the mountains.

Every photograph seemed to open a window into another world.

But travelling has taught me something photographs never can:

A picture can show you a mountain, but it cannot tell you what it feels like to place your feet upon it.

It can capture a waterfall, but not the way its endless roar echoes inside your chest.

It can freeze a cloud in a frame, but it cannot tell you what it feels like when that cloud suddenly drifts close enough to touch.

So, one morning, before the sun had risen, I packed my backpack, picked up my camera, and set out on another journey.

Because some destinations were never meant to be seen through a screen.

They were meant to be **felt—one step at a time.**

---

## Through the Heart of Bangladesh

For me, every memorable journey somehow feels more complete when it begins with a train.

There is something timeless about travelling by rail.

As the train slowly pulled away from Dhaka, the concrete walls and crowded skyline gradually disappeared behind me.

And then came the green.

Endless green.

Rice fields stretching toward the horizon. Quiet ponds reflecting the sky. Narrow rivers cutting through villages. Small houses resting beneath trees, their courtyards carrying the ordinary rhythm of everyday life.

As the train passed through villages, children waved from the roadside.

Perhaps they had no idea that someone sitting behind that window might remember those little waves for years.

A farmer paused his work for a moment and looked toward the passing train before returning to his field.

Small railway stations appeared and disappeared one after another.

Each seemed to have its own rhythm.

And I simply sat by the window, my camera resting beside me, watching Bangladesh unfold.

That morning, I felt as though I were seeing my own country for the first time.

And somewhere between two stations, a thought quietly came to me—

**Perhaps the beauty of a journey does not begin when we reach our destination.**

Sometimes, the most beautiful part of the journey is the view between two stations.

---

## Where the Plains Give Way to Hills

When I finally arrived in Sitakunda, the landscape seemed to change almost immediately.

The endless flatness of the plains slowly began to give way to green hills.

The air felt different.

Fresher.

Cleaner.

There was the scent of damp earth and wild trees drifting through the breeze.

The distant hills stood quietly against the sky, as though they had been there long before us and would remain long after we were gone—silent witnesses to generations of changing lives.

Sitakunda does not overwhelm you the way enormous mountain ranges sometimes do.

Its beauty is gentler.

Quieter.

It does not shout for your attention.

**It whispers its way into your heart.**

## The Climb to Chandranath

Every climb begins with confidence.

The first few steps always feel easy.

Your legs are light.

Your mind is full of excitement.

You look around, take photographs, and think,

*This isn't going to be difficult.*

But mountains have a way of reminding you that they are not interested in your confidence.

Slowly, the trail began to rise.

Smooth paths gave way to stone steps.

Each step demanded a little more effort than the one before.

My breathing grew heavier.

Sweat replaced some of the excitement I had started with.

And somewhere along the way, reaching the summit stopped being the only goal.

Now, it was simply about taking the next step.

Then another.

And another.

Every so often, I stopped.

Not because I wanted to give up.

I stopped because I wanted to look around.

Every time I turned back, the world seemed to have changed.

The path below looked smaller.

The people looked smaller.

The houses seemed even farther away.

And strangely, my worries seemed smaller too.

Standing there, I understood something simple—

**The higher you climb, the smaller your worries begin to look.**

---

## The Sound of Silence

Halfway up the mountain, I stopped again.

This time, it wasn't because I was tired.

I simply wanted to listen.

I looked around.

There were no traffic horns.

No crowded streets.

No one rushing past me.

Only the wind.

The soft movement of air through the trees.

The distant call of a bird hidden somewhere inside the forest.

The gentle brushing of one leaf against another.

And then—

silence.

For the first time in a long while, I understood that silence does not mean the absence of sound.

**Silence has a sound of its own.**

And sometimes, that sound tells you things that thousands of words never could.

---

## Above the World

When I finally reached the summit of Chandranath Hill, it did not feel exactly like victory.

It felt more like a reward.

The world suddenly opened before me.

Green hills rolled one after another toward the horizon.

Far away, the blue shimmer of the Bay of Bengal stretched beneath the sky.

The villages below looked so small that they seemed like tiny islands scattered across fields of green.

Cloud shadows moved slowly over the valleys.

I stood there quietly.

So did the others around me.

No one seemed to be in a hurry to speak.

There was no need.

Some views are not meant for conversation.

They are meant to be admired in silence.

Standing at the summit, I began to understand why people had climbed these hills for generations.

Not simply to reach the highest point.

But to see the world from somewhere else.

**To see the same world with a different perspective.**

## Following the Sound of Water

But my story in Sitakunda was far from over.

There was another side of the mountains waiting for me.

The waterfalls.

Hidden deep within forests and rocky valleys, they seemed to belong to a quieter version of the world.

Reaching them meant beginning another walk.

This time, the trail was different.

Small streams crossed our path.

The stones were wet and slippery.

Above us, enormous trees stretched their branches across the sky. Sunlight filtered through the leaves and fell upon the forest floor in soft shades of green.

It felt as though we had stepped into a part of the world untouched by the hurry of the city.

Then I heard it.

At first, only a distant sound.

A deep, steady roar.

The waterfall itself was still hidden somewhere beyond the trees.

But its presence could already be felt.

The sound grew louder with every step.

**Nature was announcing that something was waiting ahead.**

---

## The Waterfall

Then, suddenly, the trees opened.

And there it was.

Clear water rushing down dark rocks.

White streams breaking against stone before gathering in the pool below.

A fine mist rising into the cool forest air.

Tiny droplets floating through the sunlight like scattered diamonds.

I stood quietly at the edge of the water.

For a while, the sound of the waterfall was all I could hear.

And slowly, even the noise inside my own mind seemed to disappear.

There is something extraordinary about waterfalls.

They remind us how something so simple can become something unforgettable.

Water.

Gravity.

Time.

Nothing more.

And yet, together, they create a scene that can stay with you for years.

## The People Who Call the Hills Home

But there is something we should never forget when we travel.

The places we call *destinations* are often someone else's home.

For generations, people living around the hills of Sitakunda have built their lives alongside this landscape.

Small tea stalls sit beside the trails, offering tired travellers a warm cup of tea.

Local vendors sell fruit and simple food.

Families continue their ordinary routines beneath the same mountains that have watched their parents grow old and their children grow up.

And that is when travelling begins to mean something more.

A place is not merely an attraction.

It is someone's home.

Every destination belongs to someone.

Once you understand that, the way you travel begins to change.

You stop simply looking at a place.

You begin to respect the life within it.

---

## History Beneath the Landscape

Sitakunda is more than mountains, waterfalls, and green forests.

For centuries, Chandranath Hill has been an important pilgrimage site, drawing people from far and wide because of its spiritual and cultural significance.

Here, history, faith, and nature exist side by side.

Some people come seeking something spiritual.

Others come to experience the landscape.

Some simply come to spend a little time away from the noise of everyday life.

But the mountain welcomes them all in the same quiet way.

**One step at a time.**

---

## The Walk Back

The journey downhill was certainly easier.

But strangely, I walked more slowly.

The urgency I had felt while climbing was gone.

There was no summit left to chase.

So I began to notice everything I had overlooked before.

Every turn in the trail.

Every bird call.

Every movement of the wind.

Every glimpse between the trees.

Every view that had seemed ordinary only because I had been too focused on moving forward.

I wanted to remember it all.

Because I knew that once I returned to the city, these moments would no longer surround me.

They would exist only in memory.

And perhaps, someday, in the middle of a crowded afternoon, when everything around me was loud and hurried, I would suddenly remember the silence of that mountain.

The sound of that waterfall.

The coolness of that wind.

The clouds drifting across the hills.

---

## What Sitakunda Taught Me

On the journey back to Dhaka, I sat beside the train window again.

The city was slowly approaching.

But my mind was still somewhere on Chandranath Hill.

I kept thinking about the climb.

And I wondered—

Isn't life a little like climbing a mountain?

Sometimes the path is easy.

Sometimes it suddenly becomes steep.

Sometimes the destination is clearly visible.

And sometimes, the summit disappears completely from sight.

Those are the moments when you want to stop.

You wonder how much farther you have to go.

You wonder whether it is worth continuing.

But mountains teach a lesson without ever saying a word.

**The most beautiful views rarely wait at the end of the easiest roads.**

Sometimes, you have to sweat.

Sometimes, you have to struggle.

Sometimes, you have to keep walking even when you cannot see the summit.

Sometimes, all you can do is take one more step.

And then, when you finally reach the top, something unexpected happens.

You realize that you were never climbing only for the view.

**The journey itself was changing you.**

The world you see from the summit is different—not because the world has changed, but because you have.

Perhaps that is the true beauty of travelling.

We think we leave home to discover the world.

But somewhere along the way, we discover something else.

**A little more of ourselves.**

Because the greatest journeys are not measured by how far we have travelled.

They are measured by a quieter question:

**Who did we become along the way?**
